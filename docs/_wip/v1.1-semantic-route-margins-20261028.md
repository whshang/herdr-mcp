# v1.1 semantic fast-path — per-route × decision-head calibration margins

Status: **measurement recorded; no threshold, deadline, prompt or production change made.**
Issue: #622 ("semantic fast-path: calibration margins vs the fixed threshold contract").
Measured: 2026-10-28, commit `dbd2a923eea4dcdeecf1fff6e6800c6559a4509b`, runtime `1.1.0-rc.3`.
Supersedes the margin gap left open by the T3 re-measure
(`docs/history/architecture/v1.1-jev-remeasure-20261011.md`), which reported p50/p95/max per route
and the share of calls inside the production attempt deadline, but not a per route × head margin.

## What this measures

The semantic stage has two decision heads, and each is charged against a different production
budget (`crates/herdr-mcp/src/semantic.rs`, `mcp.rs`):

| decision head | who consumes it | attempt budget | pool budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| typed fast path (`decision`, `decision-vercel`) | Runtime/browser fast path, goal supervisor | 1800 ms | 3600 ms |
| chat fallback (`openai-chat`) | extension supervisor/judge fallback | 5000 ms | 12000 ms |

**Margin** is the headroom each measured call keeps against the budget it is charged to:

- `attempt margin = attempt budget − max observed latency`
- `max / attempt` = observed share of the attempt budget
- **margin exhausted** = at least one call reached or exceeded the budget. A call *at* the budget
  counts as exhausted, because production abandons the attempt when the deadline is reached.

Every measured call is counted, successful or not: a failed or timed-out call consumes the same
attempt budget as a success, and a timeout is the most margin-relevant observation a route can
produce. Only `case_invalid` samples are excluded, because they never reached a provider.

Note the bench's own per-call timeout (`--attempt-ms`, here 15000 ms) is deliberately **wider** than
the production attempt budget. That is what makes the margin visible: a call that succeeds in the
bench at 2387 ms would have been abandoned at 1800 ms in production. The bench measures how much
latency the providers actually deliver; the margin says how that fits the shipped deadline.

This is a *decision-head* margin. It is not the per-question-head agreement table (which is about
answer correctness per Jev question, not latency).

## Method

Run on the workstation whose `~/.config/herdr-mcp/config.json` defines 8 semantic routes
(5 typed: `jev-whshang`, `jev-whshang1`, `jev-whshangasu`, `jev-openrouter`, `jev-vercel`;
3 chat: `llm-qingxian`, `llm-ollama`, `llm-whshang`). Route credentials are read in memory by the
Rust harness only and are never copied into an artifact; recorded samples carry route name,
protocol, model, case id, latency, error code and the provider's own answer.

Command:

```bash
CARGO_TARGET_DIR=/Users/qingxian/.herdr/cargo-target-split666 \
node scripts/bench/run-semantic-bench.mjs --results-dir=/tmp/semantic-bench-622 --runs=3
```

- 12 typed cases + 7 chat cases from `tests/bench/fixtures/semantic-cases.json`, the same cases for
  every route; `--runs=3` matches the earlier T3 re-measure (36 typed calls, 21 chat calls per route).
- Strictly sequential, one route at a time, 600 ms pacing between typed calls and 1200 ms between
  chat calls. A route returning HTTP 429 or failing three times in a row stops early and is reported
  with a `stopped` reason.
- Measurement runs inside `crates/herdr-mcp/src/semantic_bench.rs` (test-only, `--ignored`), so the
  shipped providers, parsers and timeouts are what gets measured.

Raw per-route samples: `/tmp/semantic-bench-622/route-*.json` and `samples-merged.json`.
Every number below is taken from that run; none are estimated.

Rows for `jev-vercel` (row 5) and `llm-ollama` (row 7) describe **failing** calls only — neither has a
successful call in this run — so their margins say nothing about success latency and must not be read
as headroom. They are included so the table accounts for every measured call.

One deliberate difference from the route table above is not a bug: the route table's `max` and
`≤ attempt` columns are computed over **successful** samples only (`llm-whshang` shows max 6876 ms and
90.0% ≤ attempt), while the margin table counts **every** measured call, so the same route shows
15004 ms and a pool-exhausted flag once its single timeout is included. Both readings are correct and
answer different questions — "how fast is this route when it answers" versus "what does this route
actually cost the budget, including the call that never answered" — and the margin question is the one
this report exists to answer.

## Analysis

### Typed fast path (1800 ms attempt / 3600 ms pool)

The typical margin is large. On the four working typed routes p50 is 285–487 ms and p90 is
328–605 ms, i.e. **3.0×–5.5× inside** the attempt budget at p90, and every route's p90 clears the
pool budget as well. A representative typed decision costs ~0.3 s against a 1.8 s allowance.

The tail is thin but real, and it is the actual finding of this measurement: two routes produced
**exactly one successful call each that still exceeded the attempt budget** — `jev-whshang1` at
2387 ms (132.6% of budget) and `jev-whshangasu` at 2066 ms (114.8%). These are not errors; the
provider answered. In production they would be discarded at 1800 ms and the pool would fail over to
the next route.

So the typed head's calibration reads as: **median margin enormous, p90 margin comfortable,
~2.8% (1/36) of calls over the attempt line on two of four routes.** The pool budget (3600 ms) is
never approached by a typed call: the worst is 2387 ms, leaving 1213 ms of pool headroom.

### Chat fallback (5000 ms attempt / 12000 ms pool)

The chat margin is materially tighter and is the one place this measurement finds a real deadline
question.

- `llm-qingxian` — healthy: p50 1800 ms, p90 2345 ms, max 3320 ms, i.e. 66.4% of the attempt
  budget at its worst call, with 8680 ms of pool headroom.
- `llm-whshang` — the thinnest margin on the whole configuration. p50 2027 ms is fine, but p90 is
  6226 ms (**over** the 5000 ms attempt budget), one call timed out at the 15000 ms harness ceiling
  (15004 ms recorded), and max therefore also **exceeds the 12000 ms pool budget** (`pool margin`
  is −3004 ms, flagged `yes — pool ×1`). Unlike the typed tail, this is not a single outlier inside a
  comfortable distribution: the p90 itself is past the attempt line, so ~10% of `llm-whshang` chat
  calls are expected to be discarded and failed over.
- `llm-ollama` — **no latency evidence**: every call returned HTTP 429 and the route stopped with
  `rate_limited` after 3 samples.

### Two failures in this run are route/credential problems, not calibration

- `jev-vercel` returned HTTP 403 on all 3 calls (`provider_error_streak`), reproducing F2 from the
  T3 re-measure: the Vercel AI Gateway entitlement for this route is still not usable, and the
  `decision-vercel` parser remains unverified against live traffic. A 403 costs one attempt and then
  cooldown; failover is correct and bounded. **This is not a threshold signal.**
- `llm-ollama` returned HTTP 429 on all 3 calls (`rate_limited`). The T3 re-measure had this route at
  21/21 within budget (`p50 1734 ms`, `max 2756 ms`), so the route's usable capacity changed between
  the two runs. This matches the issue's own note that one chat-route deadline miss "was traced to a
  route or credential choice rather than to the threshold". **Also not a threshold signal** — it is a
  quota/credential condition that must be repaired before this route can be measured at all.

## Do the thresholds or deadlines need tuning?

**No threshold or deadline change is justified by this measurement, and none was made.** Reasoning,
recorded as required by the issue:

1. **The fixed threshold contract is untouched and unexercised by these numbers.** Margins here are
   latency-vs-deadline, not probability-vs-threshold. The `>= 0.80 true / <= 0.20 false / otherwise
   uncertain` contract is unchanged, and the fail-safe fallback (uncertainty preserves deterministic
   policy) is unchanged. This measurement produced no evidence for moving it.
2. **The typed 1800 ms attempt deadline has healthy headroom where it counts.** p90 is 3.0×–5.5×
   inside budget on every working typed route. Two routes each show a single over-budget success,
   which the 3600 ms pool and route failover already absorb by design. Raising the typed deadline to
   cover a 2.8% tail would widen the worst-case fast-path latency for every call to serve a rare
   event the pool already bounds — a worse trade than leaving it.
3. **The chat 5000 ms attempt deadline is the genuine candidate, but it is a route problem first.**
   `llm-whshang`'s p90 (6226 ms) is past the attempt budget, which suggests either the route is
   undersized for this workload or the 5 s attempt allowance is optimistic for `deepseek-v4-flash`.
   Two things must happen before a deadline change could be justified: (a) the route must be measured
   again alongside a healthy `llm-ollama`, so we know whether the 5 s line is wrong in general or
   only on this one model, and (b) it must be established that the pool/failover bound is actually
   being exceeded in production, rather than merely that one route is slow. Per the reviewer's
   standing instruction, tuning from a single slow/failing route is exactly what must not be done.
4. **The 403 and 429 routes carry no tuning signal at all.** Credential/quota failures are repaired
   or the route is removed; they never argue for moving a deadline.

Recorded recommendation: **leave the thresholds and both attempt/pool deadlines as they are**, repair
`jev-vercel` (credential/entitlement) and `llm-ollama` (quota) first, and re-measure chat with all
three fallback routes usable before reconsidering the 5000 ms chat attempt allowance. `llm-whshang`
is the route to watch.

## Observed but out of scope

Answer accuracy on this run was lower than the T3 re-measure for the typed head (66.7%–69.4% vs
80.6%–83.3%), concentrated in two heads: `can_continue` (36.1% agreement) and
`useful_work_remaining` (36.1%), both failing as `uncertain` at 0.31–0.36 (false side) and
0.77–0.79 (true side) — i.e. sitting just outside the `<= 0.20 / >= 0.80` gates.
`task_completed` also produced 0.77–0.79 on its true side. That is the same noul-boundary pattern the
T3 re-measure recorded as F4, now wider on `can_continue` and `useful_work_remaining`; `can_continue`
was a single 0.79 sample before and is 13/36 now. This is a **correctness/threshold-margin
observation for whoever owns the Jev model or prompt**, recorded here because it was measured, and it
is explicitly **not** acted on in this change: no prompt, threshold or model was touched.

## Deterministic coverage of the boundary handling

The margin report's boundary behaviour is covered by an automated test rather than by eyeballing live
numbers: `tests/bench/fixtures/margin-boundary-samples.json` plus two tests in
`tests/bench-runner.test.mjs`.

Those fixture numbers are **synthetic recorded samples, not measurements**. The fixture exists only
to pin the arithmetic at the boundaries: a call just under the budget, exactly at it (margin 0,
flagged exhausted), just over it (negative margin), and exactly at the pool boundary. The committed
fixture lists its own `boundaries` block and its models are named
`fixture-not-a-measurement` so they cannot be mistaken for route evidence.

## What is not covered

- **One measurement day, one workstation, one model set.** These are single-run latencies on
  2026-10-28; there is no day-over-day or cross-workstation sample, so the tail rates (1/36) carry
  no confidence interval.
- **`llm-ollama` was not measured** (429 on every call), so the chat fallback head is characterised by
  2 of its 3 routes.
- **`jev-vercel` has no successful call**, so `decision-vercel` latency remains unmeasured and the
  parser still unverified live.
- **No production traffic is observed.** Margins are provider-delivery latency under bench pacing, not
  the production distribution, and the pool/failover interaction under real concurrency is not
  exercised.
- The `15004 ms` sample is the harness's own 15000 ms ceiling, not a provider-reported latency.
